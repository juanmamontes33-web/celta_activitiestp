OVERVIEW
The three parts
Familiar → abstract 🪜
Part 1 (4–5 min) — questions about you: home, work, hobbies.
Part 2 (3–4 min) — the long turn: 1 min to prepare, speak 1–2 min on a cue card.
Part 3 (4–5 min) — discussion of abstract ideas linked to the Part 2 topic.

GOLDEN RULE
Never stop at one word
Answer → reason → example 🌱
Too short
"Do you like cooking?" — "Yes."
Band 7
"Yes, I love it, because it helps me relax. For example, I made paella last weekend — it was really satisfying."
English from Glory · IELTS · Speaking03 / 10
FLUENCY
Buy thinking time
Fillers beat silence 🗣️
"That's an interesting question…"
"Well, let me think for a second…"
"I'd say it depends on…"
"To be honest, …"
⚠️ A natural filler keeps fluency flowing; a long silent pause breaks it.

PART 2
The long turn · the cue card
One minute to plan, then speak 🎤
Cue card:
Describe a skill you would like to learn. Say what it is · why you want it · how you'd learn it · and why it would be useful.
💡 Note keywords, not sentences. Cover all four bullets. Keep talking until the examiner stops you.
English from Glory · IELTS · Speaking05 / 10
PART 3
The discussion
Think bigger & more abstract 🌍
Questions widen out: "Should schools teach more practical skills?"
Use opinion + reason + example, and hedge: "It may be partly because…"
Compare, speculate, and consider both sides before concluding.

MARKING
How it's scored
Four equal criteria 🏆
Fluency & Coherence — smooth, connected speech.
Lexical Resource — range of vocabulary & collocation.
Grammatical Range & Accuracy — varied, correct structures.
Pronunciation — clear, with natural stress & intonation.

WATCH OUT
Common issues · Spanish L1
Pronunciation & habits 🚫
Don't add /e/ before s-clusters: "estudy" → "study".
Pronounce final -s and -ed endings clearly.
Don't memorise scripts — speak naturally; examiners hear rehearsed answers.
