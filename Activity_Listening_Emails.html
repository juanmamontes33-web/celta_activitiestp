📝 Key facts — teacher reference

Speaker: Erica Dhawan, communication expert & author of Digital Body Language.
Core idea: in person, 60–80% of meaning comes from body language (tone, pauses, gestures). Online, we send "digital body language" cues — often by accident or wrong.
The problem: up to 50% of the time, the tone of an email is misread. Very short messages (like "Call me") feel cryptic, so readers overthink and get stressed.
Her advice: watch your tone (especially when rushing), limit all-caps and emojis, and re-read before you send.
Her mantra: "Reading carefully is the new listening; writing clearly is the new empathy."

Target language

Email chunks

Functional email phrases, with MFP: Meaning · Form · Pronunciation. (Use these to rewrite unclear emails!)

📨 Opening & purpose

Dear Mr / Ms …, · Hi …,
I hope you're well.
I'm writing to …
M greet + say why.  F I'm writing to + base verb.  P weak to /tə/.

🙏 Polite requests

Could you please …?
I was wondering if you could …
Would it be possible to …?
M ask politely.  F Could/Would + base verb.  P soft tone; could /kəd/.

📎 Attachments & referring

Please find attached …
I've attached …
As mentioned, …
M point to a file / earlier point.  F find attached + noun.  P at·TACHED /əˈtætʃt/.

🔁 Follow-ups & closing

I'm just following up on …
I look forward to hearing from you.
Best regards, / Kind regards,
M chase a reply / sign off.  F look forward to + -ing (gerund!).  P "look forward to HEARing".
