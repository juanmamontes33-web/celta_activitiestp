📝 Key facts — teacher reference

Programme: BBC 6 Minute English, "The future of transport" (Rob & Neil, ~6 min).
Automation = machines doing work humans normally do → driverless vehicles. Alstom (France) is testing automated freight trains; the prototype runs ~100 km without a driver.
Hyperloop One (tested in Nevada, USA): you're loaded into a pod and pushed through a vacuum tube at up to 1,123 km/h. Engineer Anita Sengupta calls it a maglev train in a vacuum.
maglev = magnetic levitation (trains on a magnetic track, not rails). vacuum = a space with all air removed → no resistance → energy-efficient.
Quiz: the fastest ever recorded train is a maglev from the Central Japan Railway Company — 603 km/h on a test track (so the answer is Japan, not China).
Vocabulary: commonplace · automation · prototype · far-fetched · maglev · vacuum.

Target language

Getting around & directions

Real phrases for finding your way, with MFP: Meaning · Form · Pronunciation. (Use these to role-play in the city!)

🙋 Asking for directions

Excuse me, how do I get to …?
Is there a … near here?
Is it far? / How long does it take?
M ask the way politely.  F how do I get to + place.  P weak to /tə/; start with "Excuse me".

➡️ Giving directions

Go straight on.
Turn left / right at …
It's on your left, opposite the …
M tell someone the way.  F imperatives: Go / Turn / Take + …  P stress the direction: LEFT, RIGHT.

🚇 Public transport

Which line / bus goes to …?
Where do I change?
Does this train stop at …?
M use the network.  F Which … goes to + place?  P Which /wɪtʃ/; rising tone on the question.

🔁 Checking & clarifying

Sorry, could you repeat that?
So it's the second left?
Did you say left or right?
M make sure you understood.  F So it's … ? (confirm).  P fall–rise = polite checking.
