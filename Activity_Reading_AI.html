Your Invisible Assistant

Artificial Intelligence

1Twenty years ago, "artificial intelligence" sounded like science fiction. Today it is almost invisible — because it is everywhere. When your phone suggests the fastest route home, finishes your sentence in a message, or recommends the next song, that is AI quietly doing its job.

2So what is it, exactly? In simple terms, AI is software that has been trained on huge amounts of data until it can recognise patterns and make decisions. A chatbot that answers your questions, a camera that unlocks your phone by looking at your face, a bank system that spots a strange payment — all of these are powered by the same basic idea.

3The appeal is obvious. AI can work faster than any human, it never gets tired, and for many routine tasks it is more accurate than we are. Businesses use it to automate repetitive jobs so that people can spend their time on more creative work.

4But there are worries too. If machines can be trained to do our tasks, some jobs may disappear. People can also rely on AI too much and stop thinking for themselves. And AI is not perfect: it learns from data made by humans, so it can repeat our mistakes — and sometimes it is confidently wrong.

5Most experts agree on one thing: AI is a tool, not a boss. Used well, it can save time and open doors. The important question is not whether the technology is clever, but whether we stay in control of it.
