IELTS Academic · Writing Task 2
The opinion essay ✍️
40 minutes, 250+ words, worth twice Task 1. A clear position, argued across four paragraphs.
English from Glory · IELTS · Writing Task 201 / 10
LEAD-IN
Read the question type
Answer the exact question 🎯
Agree / disagree — "To what extent do you agree?" → take a position.
Discuss both views — "Discuss both views and give your opinion."
Problem / solution — "What problems… and what solutions?"
Advantages / disadvantages — "Do the benefits outweigh…?"
⚠️ Wrong question type = low Task Response, whatever the English quality.
English from Glory · IELTS · Writing Task 202 / 10
STRUCTURE
The 4-paragraph shape
Four paragraphs 🧱
Introduction — paraphrase the prompt + state your position (thesis).
Body 1 — topic sentence → reason → example.
Body 2 — topic sentence → reason → example.
Conclusion — restate your position + summarise.
English from Glory · IELTS · Writing Task 203 / 10
INTRO
Write the introduction
Paraphrase, then commit 🎬
Prompt: Children should start school at a very early age. Agree?
"It is sometimes argued that formal education should begin in early childhood. While there are clear benefits, I would argue that the drawbacks are more significant."
💡 Don't copy the prompt — paraphrase it. State your position in the intro, not just the conclusion.
English from Glory · IELTS · Writing Task 204 / 10
BODY
Develop each idea
Topic → Reason → Example 🔗
Topic sentence — "One major advantage is social development."
Reason — "This is because young learners who mix with peers…"
Example — "For instance, studies of nursery children show…"
🎯 Every claim needs support. One well-developed idea beats three undeveloped ones.

WATCH OUT
Common errors · Spanish L1
Fix these 🚫
✗ "Nowadays the people think…" → ✓ Nowadays, people think… (no the)
✗ "In my personal opinion I think…" → ✓ In my opinion, … (don't double up)
✗ "Is important to…" → ✓ It is important to… (dummy it)

IELTS Task 2 — in a nutshell 🥜
Read the question type → answer exactly what it asks.
Four paragraphs: intro (paraphrase + thesis), 2 bodies, conclusion.
Every claim: topic sentence → reason → example; hedge your claims.
