B2 First Speaking — run sheet & script

Core 5 · Class 74. ~14 minutes. Standard exam: 2 candidates, 2 examiners (an interlocutor who talks, and an assessor who only listens). Teaching online 1-to-1? You play the interlocutor and the partner — or pair two students in the main room. Use the activity's prompt cards; share the Part 2 photos on screen.
P1 ~2 minP2 ~4 minP3 ~4 minP4 ~4 min

▶ Stage-by-stage

Part 1 · Interview2 min · T–S, then S–S echo
"Good morning. Can you tell me your names? … Where are you from, {name}? … Do you work or study? … What do you enjoy doing at the weekend? … And what are your plans for the future?"
Look for: extended answers (answer + reason + example), not one-word replies. Nudge: "Can you tell me a bit more?" Jot errors; don't correct yet.
Part 2 · Long turn4 min · 1 min each + 30s response
"Here are your two photographs. They show people studying in different ways. I'd like you to compare them and say why the people might have chosen these ways of studying. You have one minute." … (after) To partner: "Which way would you prefer? (about 30 seconds)."
Look for: comparing + speculating (not listing every detail), language stretched to the full minute. Do not interrupt. Then repeat with a new photo pair for the other candidate.
Part 3 · Collaborative task4 min · S–S discuss (2m) + decide (1m)
"Now, talk together. Here are some ways people can improve their English (watching series · a course · travelling · apps · speaking with friends). Talk to each other about how effective each one is. You have two minutes." … (after 2 min) "Now you have about a minute to decide which two are the most effective."
Look for: interaction — asking the partner's view, building on ideas, negotiating ("Shall we…? / I see your point, but…"). Step back and let them run it.
Part 4 · Discussion4 min · T–S & S–S
"Is it better to learn a language as a child or as an adult? … Do you think technology will ever replace language teachers? … Why do some people find speaking harder than writing?"
Look for: developed opinions with reasons/examples, reacting to each other, and range — conditionals, modals, reported speech. Address both candidates; invite one to respond to the other.

★ What you're assessing

Grammar & Vocabulary

Range and accuracy. Reward Core 5 structures used naturally.

Discourse Management

Length of turns, coherence, linking words, not drying up.

Pronunciation

Word/sentence stress and intonation — intelligible, not accent-free.

Interactive Communication

Turn-taking, initiating, responding, keeping the talk going (esp. Part 3).

✎ Correction & feedback

Delayed correction
  • Never interrupt the Part 2 long turn or a Part 3 exchange — note errors silently.
  • After the full round, run a delayed feedback board: 4–6 errors, elicit corrections from the pair.
  • Praise one strong exam phrase each candidate used, and give one concrete target (e.g. "ask your partner's opinion in Part 3").
  • Then re-run the weakest part once — Test → Teach → Test.

💻 Online delivery (Zoom / Meet)

Setup
  • Share the two Part 2 photos full-screen; give the exact rubric, then mute yourself and time 1 minute.
  • For Part 3, display the five options on screen; let the students lead — turn your camera into "listener" mode.
  • Use a visible on-screen timer so candidates pace themselves.
  • 1-to-1? You play the partner, but keep your turns short so the student speaks ~70% of the time.
