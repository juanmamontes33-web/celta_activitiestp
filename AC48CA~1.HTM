Countable & uncountable nouns + quantifiers

Talking about food, drink & amounts — some/any · much/many · a little/a few · too much/too many

🔢 Countable vs uncountable

Countable: an egg, two eggs, a plate. Uncountable: rice, water, bread, sugar, cheese, food — no plural, singular verb.

➕ some / any

some in positives & offers; any in negatives & questions. I'd like some water. Is there any bread? There isn't any milk.

❓ much / many

much + uncountable; many + countable (mostly in ?/–). How much sugar? How many chips? Not much time, not many people.

⚖️ a little / a few · too much / too many

a little + uncountable, a few + countable (= a small amount). too much/too many = more than you want.

📝 Quick reference — partitives (a … of)

To count uncountable things, use a "container/portion" word: a glass of water · a bottle of wine · a cup of coffee · a slice/loaf of bread · a piece of cheese · a bit of salt · a bowl of soup.

MFP

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

The four quantifier pairs, clarified with Meaning · Form · Pronunciation.

some / any

I'd like some tea.
Is there any milk?
We don't have any bread.
M an unspecified amount.  F some = +/offers; any = –/?.  P weak some /səm/, any /ˈeni/.

much / many

How much rice? (uncount.)
How many eggs? (count.)
Not much / not many …
M ask/talk about quantity.  F much + uncount; many + count.  P much /mʌtʃ/, many /ˈmeni/.

a little / a few

a little sugar (uncount.)
a few olives (count.)
= a small amount
M a small (positive) amount.  F a little + uncount; a few + count.  P weak a /ə/.

too much / too many

too much salt (uncount.)
too many plates (count.)
= more than you want
M an excessive amount.  F too much + uncount; too many + count.  P stress TOO.
