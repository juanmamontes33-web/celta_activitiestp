FORM
Obligation & advice · the toolkit
OBLIGATION
must — I feel it's necessary
have to — an outside rule
have got to — informal
"I must call her." / "I have to work."
ADVICE
should / ought to — recommend
had better — strong advice / warning
'd better not — warning (neg)
"You should rest." / "You'd better hurry!"
🔑 No to after must / should / had better: "must go", "should go", "had better go". Only have to and ought to keep it.

KEY CONCEPT
The trap · forbidden vs not necessary
MUSTN'T = 🚫 DON'T DO IT
"You mustn't use your phone in the exam."
It's forbidden / prohibited. (= You are not allowed to.)
DON'T HAVE TO = 🆓 NOT NECESSARY
"You don't have to bring a pen; we provide them."
There's no obligation — but you can if you want.
🇪🇸 In Spanish "no tienes que" can feel like both — in English the meanings are opposite. This is a classic B2 First trap.

C1 STRETCH
⛰️ Push to C1 · subtler obligation
More ways to oblige 🔼
be supposed to — expectation, often broken: "You're supposed to book ahead."
be to — formal rules/orders: "Visitors are to sign in."
should have + V3 — past criticism/regret: "You should have told me."
🗣️ Criticise one past decision with "You should have…".

TAKEAWAY
The rules to keep!
Modals — in a nutshell 🥜
must / have to = obligation · should / ought to / had better = advice.
mustn't = forbidden · don't have to = not necessary. Opposite meanings!
No to after must / should / had better.
