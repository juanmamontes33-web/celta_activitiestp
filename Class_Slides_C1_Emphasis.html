FORM · INVERSION
Negative adverbial + question word order
Never / Rarely / Seldom + auxiliary + subject…
"Rarely do we see such talent."
Not only did he win, but he also…
Hardly had I arrived when…
No sooner had it begun than…
Little did they know…
🔑 Treat it like a question: Never have I… (not "Never I have"). Pairs: Hardly…when, No sooner…than.

FORM · CLEFT
Split the sentence to spotlight one part
IT-CLEFT
"It was her honesty that impressed me."
It + be + the focus + that/who… Highlights a noun, a time or a place.
WHAT-CLEFT
"What I need is a long holiday."
What + clause + be + the focus. Great for openings and summaries.
🎯 Use clefts to control emphasis in writing: "What matters most is…" is a powerful essay opener.

Emphasis — in a nutshell 🥜
Inversion: front a negative (never, rarely, not only, hardly, no sooner) → auxiliary before subject.
It-cleft: It + be + focus + that… · What-cleft: What + clause + be + focus.
Use them sparingly and accurately — one per paragraph is plenty.
