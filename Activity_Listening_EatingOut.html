📝 Key facts — teacher reference

Programme: BBC 6 Minute English, "The hidden life of buffets" (Neil & Sam, ~6 min, 2023).
Buffet = an all-you-can-eat meal; a feast of many dishes. Popularity is booming, especially in Middle Eastern & Asian countries — but buffets are criticised for waste.
Quiz: the word 'buffet' is French (the serving table), but buffets are thought to come from Sweden, in the Middle Ages.
John Wood (Kitchen Cut) ran a 1,000-seat breakfast buffet at the five-star Jumeirah Beach Hotel in Dubai. Diners want to get their money's worth, so they pile plates high with the most expensive food.
Sandeep Sreedharan (a wedding caterer in Goa, India): rich weddings have huge buffets — up to 5,000 guests — designed to knock your socks off. Each must top the last — a vicious circle.
Vocabulary: eyes bigger than your belly/stomach · pile up · get your money's worth · a vicious circle · knock your socks off · a caterer.

Target language

Eating out

Restaurant phrases, with MFP: Meaning · Form · Pronunciation. (Use these to role-play ordering a meal!)

🍽️ Ordering

I'll have …
Could I get …?
For me, the … please.
M say what you want.  F I'll have + noun.  P I'll /aɪl/; weak the /ðə/.

📋 Asking about the menu

What do you recommend?
Does it come with …?
What's the … like?
M ask before you choose.  F Does it come with + noun?  P recommend → re·co·MMEND.

🥗 Dietary needs

I'm allergic to …
Could I have it without …?
Is this vegetarian / vegan?
M stay safe / adapt a dish.  F allergic to + noun (NOT of).  P a·LLER·gic /əˈlɜːdʒɪk/.

💳 Paying

Could we have the bill, please?
Can I get this to go?
Is service included?
M finish & pay.  F Could we have + noun?  P bill (UK) / check (US); polite fall–rise.
