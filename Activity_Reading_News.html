What's Going On?

Media

1Not long ago, most people got their news from a newspaper or the evening television bulletin. Today, the news comes to us all day long — on our phones, in our social media feeds, from friends in messaging groups. We are more informed than ever before. But we are also, strangely, more confused.

2The problem is fake news: false stories that are designed to look exactly like real news. Some are created as jokes; others are made to make money or to change what people think. Studies suggest that false stories often spread faster than true ones, because they are more shocking, and shocking things get more clicks.

3So how can you tell what to trust? Experts give simple advice. First, check the source: is it a reliable news organisation, or a website you have never heard of? Second, look at the writing. Lots of capital letters, spelling mistakes and dramatic language are warning signs. Third, and most importantly, cross-check the story with other sources. If only one strange website is reporting something huge, be suspicious.

4Images can lie too. A real photograph can be used with a completely false story, so the same picture may appear in several unrelated articles. A quick reverse image search often reveals the truth in seconds.

5None of this means we should stop reading the news. It means we should read it like detectives — asking questions, staying calm, and thinking before we share. In a world full of information, knowing how to check it is one of the most valuable skills you can have.
