THE LAUREL
EMPHASIS · INVERSION & CLEFT
Inversion — a negative adverbial, then question word order
Never have I seen such a view. · Not only did he apologise, but he also paid.
Cleft — split a sentence to spotlight one part
It was her honesty that impressed me. · What I need is a break.
👉 These structures add emphasis and formality — exactly the range that lifts a C1 essay or speaking answer. After a fronted negative (never, rarely, not only, hardly, no sooner, little), invert: auxiliary + subject.

🎓 Where it scores

This is prime range language. In IELTS Writing Task 2 and Speaking, one well-placed cleft or inversion lifts your Grammatical Range band. In Cambridge C1 Advanced, inversions are a favourite in Use of English Part 4 (key word transformations) — e.g. "No sooner had… than…".
