📄 Audio script — teacher reads aloud / post-listening reference

HOST: Welcome back to The Signal. Today's big question: will a robot take your job? Every week someone asks me if a machine is going to replace them. What's the honest answer?

GUEST: The honest answer is: some tasks, yes; most whole jobs, no. AI is very good at repetitive, predictable work — sorting data, basic translation, simple customer questions. Those tasks are already being automated.

HOST: So which jobs are the safest?

GUEST: Jobs that need the things machines are still bad at: creativity, empathy, judgement, working with people. A nurse, a teacher, a good manager — these are hard to automate, because they depend on human connection.

HOST: But we keep hearing that millions of jobs will disappear.

GUEST: Some will. History shows, though, that technology also creates new jobs we can't yet imagine. Thirty years ago nobody was a social media manager. The real risk isn't that robots take every job — it's that people who don't adapt get left behind.

HOST: So what should our listeners actually do?

GUEST: Three things. First, learn to use AI tools rather than fear them — the person who uses AI will replace the person who doesn't. Second, build the human skills: communication, problem-solving, teamwork. Third, keep learning. The most valuable skill now is the ability to learn something new quickly.

HOST: And is there any good news for workers?

GUEST: Plenty. If machines take the boring parts of our jobs, we're free to do more interesting, creative work. Used well, AI could give us better jobs, not no jobs.

HOST: A hopeful note to end on. Thanks for joining us on The Signal.
