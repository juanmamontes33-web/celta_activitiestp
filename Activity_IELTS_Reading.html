📖 The Honeybee's Dance
Honeybees communicate the location of food through a remarkable behaviour known as the 'waggle dance'. When a forager returns to the hive having found a rich source of nectar, it performs a figure-of-eight movement on the vertical surface of the honeycomb. The angle of the dance relative to vertical indicates the direction of the food in relation to the sun, while the duration of the 'waggle' phase signals the distance — longer waggles mean the food is further away. The dance was first decoded by the Austrian scientist Karl von Frisch in the 1940s, work for which he later received a Nobel Prize. Remarkably, other bees interpret the dance in the darkness of the hive, relying on touch and the vibrations it produces. Not all bee species dance, however; some simpler species rely instead on scent trails to guide their nestmates.

🎓 Band booster

Golden rule for TFNG: FALSE means the passage says the opposite; NOT GIVEN means the passage simply doesn't say. If you can't find it, it's NOT GIVEN — don't use outside knowledge. Timing: ~20 min per passage; don't get stuck — mark a guess and move on. Questions usually follow the order of the text.
