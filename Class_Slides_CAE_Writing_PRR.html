THE CHOICE
You pick one
Three common genres 🎲
Proposal — forward-looking, persuasive, recommends action to a decision-maker.
Report — factual and neutral, presents findings on a past situation.
Review — describes and evaluates (a film, place, product) and recommends.
🎯 Choose the genre you have the most ideas and vocabulary for — then follow its conventions exactly.
English from Glory · CAE · Writing Part 202 / 10
PROPOSAL
Looks forward · persuades
Recommend future action 🔮
Reader: a boss, principal or committee who can act.
Headings: Introduction · Current situation · Recommendations · Conclusion.
Language: "The purpose of this proposal is…", "I would strongly recommend…", "If this were implemented…".
English from Glory · CAE · Writing Part 203 / 10
REPORT
Looks back · informs
Present the findings 📊
Reader: someone who needs the facts to decide.
Headings: Introduction · Findings (by theme) · Conclusion/Recommendations.
Language: neutral — "This report aims to…", "It was found that…", "The majority of students felt…".
⚖️ Report = what is / was. Proposal = what should be. That's the key difference.
English from Glory · CAE · Writing Part 204 / 10
REVIEW
Describes · evaluates · recommends
Opinion with personality 🎬
Reader: magazine/website readers — engage them.
No headings — flowing paragraphs, a lively but controlled tone.
Language: rich adjectives, "What makes it stand out is…", and a clear final recommendation.

Before you write

Plan your headings/paragraphs, then write ~230 words.

MARKING
The four criteria
What scores well 🏆
Content — all required points covered, suited to the task.
Communicative Achievement — right genre, register & effect on reader.
Organisation — headings/paragraphs, logical flow, cohesion.
Language — range & accuracy; bring in your core-skills vocabulary.
English from Glory · CAE · Writing Part 208 / 10
WATCH OUT
Common errors
Fix these 🚫
Don't write a review as a report (or vice versa) — match the genre asked.
✗ "I recommend to install" → ✓ I recommend installing (recommend + -ing)
Don't forget headings in a proposal/report — they're part of the genre.
