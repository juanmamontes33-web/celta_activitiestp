🎙️ One-on-One Speaking Session — Run Sheet

A mixed-topic fluency class (Work & Study + Everyday Life), pitched a step above B2 comfort. Your job: keep him talking, prompt when he stalls, correct lightly at the end. Open the slides and the activity side by side.
⏱️ ~50–60 min🎯 Fluency & confidence🗣️ Student talking time: 80%+🧰 Slides + Activity

🎯 Aims & your mindset read first

Main aim: develop spoken fluency across mixed topics; build confidence producing longer, connected answers.

Your role today: be the conversation partner, not the lecturer. Talk as little as you can. Every time you're tempted to fill a silence, wait 3 more seconds first — let him produce.

  • Don't correct mid-flow. Note errors on paper and give them back at the end (delayed correction).
  • If he gives a one-word answer, bounce it back: "Interesting — why?" / "Tell me more about that."
  • Praise effort and risk-taking, not just accuracy.

① Warm-up Slide 2 · ~5 min

What to say

"Let's just chat for a bit to warm up. No pressure — full sentences when you can."

Ask the two questions. Let him ramble; this is just to get the English flowing.

If he's quiet / nervous: model it first — give YOUR answer in 3–4 sentences, then hand it back: "…that's me. What about you?"

② Round 1 — Work & Study Slide 3 · ~8 min

What to say

"Now let's talk about your work and study world. I'll ask, you talk — and give me reasons and examples."

Work through the prompts. Don't rush to the next question — dig into each one.

Prompts to go deeper

  • "Why is that the most demanding part?"
  • "Can you give me an example?"
  • "How did that make you feel?"
If he stalls: offer a sentence starter — "A typical day starts with…" and let him finish it.

③ Round 2 — Everyday Life Slide 4 · ~8 min

What to say

"Let's move into everyday life — shopping, getting around, people, news. Same idea: keep talking."

Encourage him to reuse chunks from past classes (get on with, value for money, I'd recommend…, in my opinion…). If he uses one, flag it positively: "Nice — that's exactly the phrase."

If answers are flat: add a twist — "And would you recommend it to a friend? Why?"

④ Activity — Do it, then say it Activity link · ~15 min

Open the activity. He does each task, checks it, then reads every sentence aloud. The reading-aloud is the point — don't skip it.

Task 1 — Complete & Say answer key

1. responsible · 2. sent · 3. compare · 4. to · 5. earth · 6. opinion · 7. had · 8. for
After checking: he reads all 8 full sentences aloud with natural stress. Model one if his rhythm is flat.

Task 2 — Correct & Say answer key

1. harder (not "more hard") · 2. will be held (passive — missing "be") · 3. best (not "most good") · 4. takes after (no "to") · 5. had (2nd conditional, not "will have") · 6. must be turned off (modal passive) · 7. faster (not "more fast")
After checking: he says each corrected sentence aloud. Ask "Why was it wrong?" on 1–2 of them for extra value.

Task 3 — Mini-Interview

No typing — pure speaking, grouped Work / Everyday / Opinions. Pick 4–6 questions (don't do all nine unless time allows). Push for developed answers: aim for 4+ sentences each.

Fluency stretch: after an answer, ask "Can you say that again, but add one more reason?"

Task 4 — Talk for a Minute

He presses New topic, then Start 60s, and speaks without stopping until the timer ends. Do 2–3 rounds. The goal is flow, not perfection — don't interrupt.

If he dries up before 60s: whisper a sub-question from the card to restart him. Afterwards, note 1 thing he did well + 1 to improve.

⑤ Situations / Role-play Slide 6 · ~8 min

You play the other person. Pick 2–3 cards. Keep it realistic and react naturally so he has to respond in real time.

Your opening lines (to start each one)

  • Work: "OK team, I think we should cancel the project and start again. Sound good?" (he must disagree politely)
  • Shopping: "Hello, customer service, how can I help you?" (he complains about a broken item)
  • Directions: "Excuse me! I'm completely lost — how do I get to the station?"
  • Plans: "Hey! Do you want to come over on Saturday night?" (he declines + suggests another day)
Listen for: politeness softeners (I'm afraid… / I see your point, but… / Actually…), clear sequencing in directions (go straight, turn left, it's on your right), and a counter-suggestion in the plans one.

⑥ Opinions round Slide 8 · ~6 min

Read each statement; he reacts and defends a view. Play devil's advocate — take the opposite side to force him to justify himself.

"OK, but I completely disagree — convince me."

Target language: I totally agree… / I see your point, but… / I'm not so sure, because… / It depends on…

⑦ Feedback & wrap Slide 10 · ~5 min

Delayed error correction

Now use your notes. Put 4–6 of his errors on the screen/chat without names of who said them and let him self-correct first:

"I heard a few things — can you spot what's wrong here?"
  • Praise 2 specific good moments (a great phrase, a long fluent stretch).
  • Give 1 clear focus for next time (e.g., "remember the passive: will be done, not will do").
  • Set the homework: a 1-minute English voice note about his day.

🧰 Quick support phrases keep these handy

  • To open up a short answer: "Why?" · "Tell me more." · "Give me an example."
  • To restart a stall: offer a sentence starter and let him finish it.
  • To stretch a strong answer: "Add one more reason." · "Now say it in a different way."
  • To correct kindly: echo the right form, don't lecture — "Ah, you went? …you'd been, right?"
