📝 Key facts — teacher reference

Speaker: Regina Hartley, HR executive (TED@UPS, ~9 min).
Two candidate types: the "Silver Spoon" — clear advantages, seemingly destined for success — and the "Scrapper" — someone who fought through difficulty and worked long hours against the odds (their CV may show job-hopping or a less famous college).
Her choice: she gives the Scrapper a chance — they have the grit to persist in a changing workplace.
Her line: "Choose the underestimated contender, whose secret weapons are passion and purpose. Hire the Scrapper."
The twist: the "messy" resume (given up for adoption, never finished college, dyslexia, job-hopper) belonged to Steve Jobs.

Target language

Interview-ready chunks

Talk about your job & experience, with MFP: Meaning · Form · Pronunciation. (Use these in the roleplay!)

🧩 Responsibilities

I'm responsible for …
My role involves …
I'm in charge of …
M what you do now.  F responsible for / in charge of + noun/-ing.  P weak for /fə/, of /əv/.

🏆 Achievements

I managed to …
I successfully …
I increased / improved …
M a success/result.  F managed to + base verb; past + a number.  P stress the number.

⏳ Experience

I have X years' experience in …
I've worked as a … for …
I've been …-ing since / for …
M up to now (unfinished time).  F present perfect; for + period, since + point.  P I've /aɪv/.

⚡ Power verbs

led · developed · launched · managed · increased · improved · achieved
M strong, impactful verbs.  F past + a result.  P -ed = /t/ launched, /d/ managed, /ɪd/ started.
