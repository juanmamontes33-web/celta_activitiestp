What's He Like?

People

1When we meet someone new, we form an opinion in seconds. We notice their clothes, their smile, the way they shake hands — and almost instantly we decide what kind of person they are. But first impressions can be surprisingly wrong, and the quiet person in the corner is not always shy.

2In English, we describe people in two very different ways. If you ask "What does he look like?", you want a description of appearance — tall, dark hair, glasses. But if you ask "What is he like?", you want something deeper: his personality. The two questions sound similar, yet they lead to completely different answers, and learners often mix them up.

3Personality words are some of the most useful you can learn. We call a friendly, confident person outgoing, and a calm, relaxed one laid-back. Someone practical and honest, who never shows off, is down to earth. The people we trust most are reliable — they do what they promise. And almost everyone values a good sense of humour.

4Interestingly, we often take after our parents, not just in how we look but in how we behave. A child may have her mother's patience or her father's temper. Yet personality is not fixed. The shy teenager can become a confident adult, and people frequently surprise us once we get to know them properly.

5So the next time you meet someone, enjoy your first impression — but hold it lightly. Ask questions, listen, and give people time. The person you get on with best might be the one you almost ignored.
