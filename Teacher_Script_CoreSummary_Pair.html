Teacher Script · For the teacher only

🎙️ Core Summary — Pair Speaking Run Sheet

A mixed-topic fluency review for two students (Work & Study + Everyday Life), a step above B2 comfort. Your job: set up the pairs, monitor, and only step in to unstick or to feed language. They should be talking to each other, not to you.
⏱️ ~55–65 min👥 2 students🗣️ Student talking time: 85%+🧰 Slides + Activity

🎯 Aims & your mindset read first

Main aim: spoken fluency and interaction across mixed topics; students questioning, reacting to and helping each other.

Your role today: set the task clearly, step back, and monitor. Listen in, take error notes, and keep both students balanced — don't let one dominate.

  • At the start, label them Student A and Student B — you'll reuse this all class.
  • Give crisp instructions, check with a quick ICQ, then let them run.
  • Monitor silently; note errors + good language. Correct at the end, not mid-task.
  • If one is quieter, direct a question to them by name, or make them Student A (who starts).

① Warm-up Slide 2 · ~5 min

"You two are going to do most of the talking today. Ask each other these two questions — and ask one follow-up each. Off you go."

ICQ: "Are you answering me, or your partner?" (their partner) · "How many follow-ups?" (one each)

If they finish fast: "Now tell me one interesting thing you learned about your partner."

② Round 1 — Work & Study Slide 3 · ~8 min

"Student A interviews Student B about work and study — all the questions, with follow-ups. Then swap completely."

Monitor both. Push for reasons and examples. Jot down 2–3 errors you'll use later.

If an answer is one line: feed the interviewer a follow-up — "Ask them why." Keep the pressure on the speaker, not on yourself.

③ Round 2 — Everyday Life Slide 4 · ~8 min

"Same again — swap who starts. This time, find ONE thing you have in common and ONE thing you disagree about. You'll report back."

The "report back" gives them a reason to really listen to each other. Nominate: "A, tell me what you two disagreed about."

④ Activity — Do it together, say it Activity link · ~15 min

One shared screen or both open the activity. They take turns and read answers aloud to each other.

Task 1 — Complete & Say answer key

1. responsible · 2. sent · 3. compare · 4. to · 5. earth · 6. opinion · 7. had · 8. for
Setup: A does odd numbers, B does even. After checking, each reads their sentences aloud to the other.

Task 2 — Correct & Say answer key

1. harder · 2. will be held (passive) · 3. best · 4. takes after · 5. had (2nd conditional) · 6. must be turned off (modal passive) · 7. faster
Setup: they discuss and agree each fix together. Ask one pair-check: "Why is number 2 wrong?"

Task 3 — Interview Each Other

A interviews B through one group of questions, then they swap. Push for 4+ sentence answers and a follow-up to each. Collect a "one thing in common" from them.

Task 4 — Talk for a Minute

One presses New topic + Start 60s and speaks; the partner listens and asks one follow-up at the end. Then swap. Do 2 rounds each.

Listener job: make sure the partner is genuinely listening — afterwards ask the listener to summarise in one sentence what they heard.

⑤ Role-play — A & B Slide 6 · ~10 min

They play both roles with each other (you just set it up and monitor). Pick 2–3 cards; after each, they switch A ↔ B and do it again.

Listen for: politeness softeners (I'm afraid… / I see your point, but… / Actually…), clear sequencing in directions (go straight, turn left, it's on your right), a counter-suggestion in the "plans" one, and a clear solution in the customer-service one.
If a role-play dies early: give the "difficult" partner a secret instruction — "Don't accept the first solution; push back once." This forces more language.

⑥ Mini-debates Slide 8 · ~8 min

Assign sides: A argues FOR, B argues AGAINST — then switch sides and run it again. This forces them to produce both agreeing and disagreeing language.

"A, you love working from home. B, you hate it. Convince each other. Go."

Target language: I totally agree… / I see your point, but… / I'm not so sure, because… / On the other hand…

⑦ Peer feedback & wrap Slide 10 · ~6 min

Delayed error correction

Put 4–6 errors you heard on the screen/chat without saying who said them. Let them self-correct as a pair first:

"Here are a few things I heard — work together, what's wrong with each?"

Peer praise

Each student tells the other one thing they did well. Then you add 1 praise + 1 focus for next time, and set the homework (1-minute voice note).

🧰 Pair-management quick tips

  • Balance talk time: if one dominates, make the quieter one Student A (the one who starts) in the next stage.
  • Stay out of it: your silence is what makes THEM talk. Monitor, don't join.
  • Nominate to check: "A, what did B say?" keeps both listening.
  • Feed, don't fix: whisper a word/phrase when stuck, then step back.
